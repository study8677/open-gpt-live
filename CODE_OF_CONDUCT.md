# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want OpenGPT Live to be a welcoming place for people with different
backgrounds, identities, experience levels, languages, and perspectives. Every
participant is expected to help keep collaboration respectful, technically
honest, and focused on improving the project.

## Expected behavior

- Be kind, patient, and specific in technical discussion.
- Critique ideas and changes, not people.
- Welcome questions and explain assumptions without gatekeeping.
- Respect privacy. Never publish another person's recordings, transcripts,
  credentials, or personal information.
- Disclose relevant conflicts of interest, especially around provider
  integrations or benchmarks.
- Accept constructive feedback and correct mistakes openly.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, discrimination, or sexualized attention.
- Personal attacks, trolling, deliberate disruption, or repeated bad-faith
  argument.
- Publishing private information without explicit permission.
- Manipulating benchmarks, hiding commercial relationships, or misrepresenting
  project capabilities.
- Any conduct that would reasonably make participation unsafe or unwelcome.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews,
and other project spaces. It also applies when someone publicly represents the
project.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report conduct concerns privately to the repository maintainers. If no private
contact route is available, open an issue requesting private contact without
including incident details or personal information.

Maintainers may remove content, issue a warning, limit participation, or ban an
account when needed to protect the community. Reports will be reviewed as
confidentially and impartially as practical. Retaliation against a reporter is
not acceptable.

## Attribution

This policy is informed by widely used open-source community standards,
including the Contributor Covenant, and is adapted to this project's focus on
voice data, provider integrations, and reproducible technical claims.
